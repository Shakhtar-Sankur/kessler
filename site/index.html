02 · Why it matters

Trust, but verify

Low Earth orbit is the busiest it has ever been, and the only collision-avoidance numbers anyone sees are the ones operators publish about themselves.

207,152
collision-avoidance maneuvers SpaceX reported for Starlink, Dec 2025 – May 2026

Starlink satellites maneuver automatically whenever the probability of a collision passes 3 in 10 million. Nobody outside SpaceX can check how often that happens.

kessler is building the tools to do it from public data: screen every pair, compute the probabilities, and detect maneuvers in public orbit histories, then compare with what operators report to their regulator.

05 · What kessler adds

An open, independent check

Built so that anyone, not only operators and governments, can see the traffic in orbit and verify what is reported about it.

  1. Every pair, every day, in public

    A daily screening of the whole active catalogue, including the Starlink–Starlink pairs that CelesTrak's SOCRATES leaves out, published as a live globe and table.

  2. GPU propagation and screening

    SGP4 at 125.7 million states a second on a free GPU, and a grid-based all-vs-all screen proven against brute force, with GPU results identical to CPU.

  3. Collision probability to NASA's standard

    Foster's 2D probability from standard Conjunction Data Messages, matching NASA CARA to 1.6e-8, and a Monte Carlo for the slow encounters where 2D fails, agreeing with CARA's on all 53 test cases at 15–19× the speed of NASA's library.

  4. A maneuver audit method

    Maneuver detection from public orbit histories, tested on debris that cannot maneuver, built to compare with operators' reports to the FCC once publication is approved.
